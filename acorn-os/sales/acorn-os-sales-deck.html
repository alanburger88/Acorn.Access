Siloed

Slow

Product — AI, governed

AI that leaves fingerprints, receipts, and an audit trail.

  • Grounded. Answers only from the document and approved knowledge — cites its sources every time.
  • Never guesses. Unsure? It says so and routes to a human — no hallucinated legal language.
  • Every action logged. Prompts, models, citations and confidence — to a tamper-evident audit trail.
  • Your data stays yours. Bring your own model; no tenant data trains models by default.
Grounded answer · cited sources · provenance
Regulated buyers are afraid of generative AI hallucinating on a legal disclosure — this slide is your answer, and it's often what gets you past the risk committee. The embedded assistant answers only from approved content and the specific document, cites its sources, and when it's unsure it refuses to guess and hands to a human. Every AI action is grounded, cited, confidence-scored and audited; no tenant data trains models by default. Contrast sharply with "AI features" bolted onto legacy tools that have no grounding and no audit. Line to use: "Our AI leaves receipts — you can prove what it did, why, and where it got the answer."

Product — author & control

Business teams author. Governance is enforced, not hoped for.

  • Author without a ticket. Drag-and-drop designer with an AI assistant that drafts copy — a human always approves.
  • Segregation of duties. The author can't approve their own work; compliance and legal sign off before anything ships.
  • Accessibility gate. A communication that fails WCAG can't be published — the system enforces it, not a policy PDF.
  • Every change versioned. Comparable, reversible, with a record of who changed what and when.
Template designer · blocks, data contract, intended outcome
Two audiences win on this slide — sell to both. Business users get a modern drag-and-drop designer with an AI assistant that drafts copy they approve: no more IT tickets for a wording change, no more six-week release for a rate update. Compliance gets teeth: segregation of duties (author ≠ approver, enforced), and an accessibility gate that literally blocks publication of anything that fails WCAG. Governance stops being a policy document nobody reads and becomes something the platform enforces at publish time. If legal or risk is in the room, slow down here — this is their slide.

Getting there

Migration is a modernization engine — not a lift-and-shift of old problems.

Ingest

AI reads your legacy estate

Inspire, Exstream, DOC1, PDFs and print streams — parsed into modern, editable templates.

Rationalize

1,400 variants → ~80 canonical

AI detects near-duplicate disclosures and collapses them into parameterized, governed content.

Verify

Parallel-run before cutover

New output is diffed against the old on real data, so you prove equivalence before you switch.

Start with one high-value communication. Expand across the estate. Migration becomes the moment you clean up — not carry forward — the mess.

De-risk the switch — this dissolves the number-one objection: "we have thousands of templates, we can't move." The Migration Studio uses AI to ingest legacy templates, PDFs and print streams, detect and collapse near-duplicates (a bank's 1,400 disclosure variants become ~80 canonical, parameterized templates), extract the variable data mappings, and generate first-draft modern templates. Parallel-run verification diffs new output against old on production data so you prove equivalence before cutover — the regulator-friendly path. This is also our land-and-expand motion: land one high-value communication, prove the outcome, expand across the estate. Migration becomes the cleanup they've wanted for years, not a risk.
